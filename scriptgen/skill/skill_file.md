# Script Revision Examples — Nick Jackson's Feedback Patterns

This document contains 8 real examples of script revision cycles between Nick Jackson (7M+ follower short-form creator) and his AI script writer. Each example shows the draft-feedback-revision loop in full detail, with Nick's exact words preserved.

The goal: anyone reading this should understand exactly WHAT Nick pushes back on, WHY he pushes back, and what the corrected version looks like.

## EXAMPLE 1: Military Draft Registration (The "CNN Hook" Problem)

**STORY:** The US government passed a law automatically registering every male citizen for the military draft starting December 2026.

**WHY THIS EXAMPLE MATTERS:** This is the most extreme revision cycle in the archive — 15+ hook rejections across 7+ rounds before approval. It reveals Nick's #1 pet peeve: scripts that sound like news reports instead of his personality.

---

HOOK A: The US government just made it so every guy in America is AUTOMATICALLY registered for the military draft — using your Social Security number.

HOOK C: The US hasn't had a draft since 1973... but they just made sure they know EXACTLY where to find you if they ever need one.

TEXT: MILITARY DRAFT? 🇺🇸🚨

> "this sounds like a cnn report on TV. this story is insane and its missing that 'artemis' vibe of reactions and chill-ness."

**WHAT HE'S SAYING:** The script reads like a news anchor explaining a policy. It's missing Nick's personality — the reactions, the casual energy, the feeling of a guy telling his friend something insane on FaceTime.

### THE SCRIPT BODY — Continued Revisions

Even after the hook was locked, the script body went through 4+ more revisions:

**Round 1 Body Feedback:**
> "the script hook and first section feels fine but the second section just seems so boring like it's an ad registration went from like 84% down to 81 that's over 1 million guys who fell off the like this is not CNN I'm Nick Jackson. **WHAT HE'S SAYING:** Registration rate statistics (84% to 81%) are policy report data. Nick's audience doesn't care about percentages. They want to know why this is insane and what it means for THEM.

**Round 2 Body Feedback:**
> "That's still really bad because of course it's not for women I mean, I said in the very beginning of the video within the first three seconds where every guy in America I don't need to clarify halfway through the video that women are not included. They know that already because I literally already said that and then the $250,000 fine up to five years in prison like obviously they're not doing it and you just clarify that so why even bring it up? It's still so pointless"

**WHAT HE'S SAYING:** Don't restate things already covered in the hook. Don't bring up facts that you immediately undercut ("there's a $250K fine... but nobody's ever been charged"). It wastes words and kills momentum.

**Round 3 Body Feedback:**
> "still absolutely horrible."

**THE BREAKTHROUGH:** The AI finally realized the middle section needed to connect to what was TRENDING — the Iran tensions happening at the same time. Instead of policy details, it connected the draft registration to the Iran situation: "Peace talks just collapsed, there's a Navy blockade on Iranian oil. And in the middle of all that, the government is making sure they know where every single guy in the country is."

### NICK'S APPROVAL
> "Wow, I really appreciate this angle. You just took with the a war and I think I'm honestly glad that we just had so many errors because that is such a smart connection to make for social media specifically [...] this entire script is just approved now ready to paste my notes like this is the perfect quality of like what I want from you of connecting the dots and being smart and tactical with how my stories are written"

### FINAL APPROVED SCRIPT

HOOK: The government just passed a law where every guy in America between 18 and 26 gets put on the military draft list, and it officially starts this December.

TEXT: MILITARY DRAFT? 🇺🇸🚨

SCRIPT:
The way it works is they pull your info from Social Security, the IRS, and your DMV. Within 30 days of turning 18, you're just on the list. Then you get a letter in the mail saying you've been registered. HELLO?

They signed this back in December, but the reason everyone is freaking out right now is because of what's happening with Iran. Peace talks just collapsed, there's a Navy blockade on Iranian oil. And in the middle of all that, the government is making sure they know where every single guy in the country is. The reason it passed is because guys just stopped signing up, so the government decided to just do it for them. OUTRO: They send you a letter AFTER you're already on the list. Appreciate the heads up.

---

### KEY LESSONS FROM THIS EXAMPLE:
1. **"CNN headline" hooks are instant kills.** Nick's hooks need to sound like something he'd actually say to a friend, not a news chyron.
2. **Don't repeat the same structure.** Writing the same hook 15 times with different words = instant frustration.
3. **The hook must roll off the tongue naturally.** Read it out loud. If the subject changes mid-sentence without a connector, it fails.
4. **Policy statistics are boring.** Nobody cares about "84% to 81%." They want to know why this is crazy.
5. **Don't restate what the hook already covered.** If the hook says "every guy" — don't clarify "women are exempt" later.
6. **Connect stories to trending events.** The Iran connection turned a policy explainer into a viral video. Always look for the bigger context.

## EXAMPLE 2: Missing Scientists Part 1 — Hook Name-Drop Formula

**STORY:** The FBI opened an investigation into 11 scientists connected to NASA, nuclear weapons, and classified programs who went missing under suspicious circumstances.

**WHY THIS EXAMPLE MATTERS:** This shows how Nick's most viral hook format was developed — the rapid-fire prestige name-drop — and how the hook went through multiple iterations to find the right "back half." The resulting video got 1.3M+ views.

---

### HOOK DEVELOPMENT

The AI presented multiple hook options. Nick immediately gravitated toward one specific structure:

> "I like this hook the best because I can see my seriousness and my tone when reading the list of the FBI, the Pentagon, etc. but I don't like how it says 'are all now investigating why America's top scientists keep going missing' so like rephrase that second bit"

The hook Nick liked the front half of:
"The FBI, the Pentagon, the White House, and Congress are ALL now investigating why America's top scientists keep going missing."

### Round 1 — Back-half options (6 variations presented)
All were different completions like "...because 11 scientists tied to NASA, nuclear weapons, and classified programs have vanished under suspicious circumstances."

### NICK'S FEEDBACK
> "OK, I don't like any of those, but give me the entire hook not just the last bit like give it all in one. It needs to be the craziest hooked to obviously get everybody's attention and make them want to watch the video."

### Round 2 — Full hooks (6 variations)

### NICK'S FEEDBACK
> "Did all of these people work for NASA and SpaceX like is that not the angle if that's true or is it something else or something bigger"

The AI clarified the scientists worked across NASA, nuclear weapons labs, military intelligence, and classified defense programs — much bigger than just NASA and SpaceX.

### Round 3 — Corrected hooks with broader umbrella

### NICK'S FEEDBACK — Near-approval
> "i feel like i love this one but the second half is a bit too long. give me options?"

The hook: "The FBI, the Pentagon, the White House, and Congress have all now officially opened an investigation — scientists connected to NASA, America's nuclear weapons programs, and classified military research keep going missing under suspicious circumstances, and the details are insane."

### Round 4 — Shortened back-half options (6 variations)

### FINAL APPROVED HOOK
"The FBI, the Pentagon, the White House, and Congress have all now officially opened an investigation — scientists connected to NASA, nuclear weapons, and classified military programs keep going missing, and the details are insane."

### KEY LESSON: The rapid-fire prestige name-drop (FBI, Pentagon, White House, Congress) is what stops the scroll. Each name carries universal authority. Every viewer knows those institutions. That's the formula.

## EXAMPLE 3: Missing Scientists Part 2 — Replicating a Viral Hook + Script Depth

**STORY:** Part 2 of the missing scientists series — focusing on WHAT the scientists were working on (antigravity, nuclear fusion, classified rocket materials, UFO testimony).

**WHY THIS EXAMPLE MATTERS:** Shows how Nick handles series videos (each must stand alone for new viewers), how he pushes back on "too technical" language, and the multi-round revision of both hook AND script body.

Options like: "Antigravity technology, nuclear fusion, classified rocket materials, and UFO testimony — the FBI is now investigating why the scientists who were working on ALL of these have gone missing."

Hook was 45 words (normal is 10-25). Solution: shortened hook ending from "and what they were actually working on makes the entire thing even crazier" to "and the details are insane" — moved the "what they were working on" concept to the on-screen TEXT instead.

### NICK'S FEEDBACK — On-screen text
> "the text is way too long. It won't fit. this text on screen goes as like a text on TikTok that I put for the hook [...] it has to be 2-4 words"

Went through many options. Nick rejected: FREE ENERGY? (isolates), ROSWELL CONNECTION? (boring), WHERE ARE THEY? (ok), THEY VANISHED? (best), CLASSIFIED SCIENTISTS? (makes no sense). Final pick: WHO WERE THEY? 🚨

### FINAL APPROVED SCRIPT

She texted a friend "if you see any report that I did this to myself, I most definitely did not." Her name was Amy Eskridge, and she was gone a month later.

An Air Force general ran the base where people believe the US government stored wreckage from a UFO that crashed in Roswell in 1947. In leaked emails, the singer of Blink-182 said this general was "very, very aware" of classified material, and a congressman tried to reach him about UFOs TWICE before he vanished. HELLO?

On Kalshi, $15 million is riding on whether the government confirms aliens exist before 2027, and 21% of people think it will.

OUTRO: I'm staying on this story, so make sure you add me because I'm going to keep you updated.

### KEY LESSONS:
1. **Each series video must stand completely alone** — new viewers who never saw Part 1 need to stop scrolling too.
2. **Technical language kills hooks** — "antigravity," "nuclear fusion," "DARPA" mean nothing to the average viewer. Use universally understood names only.
3. **Don't restate the hook** in the first paragraph of the script. Jump to NEW information immediately.
4. **Names go at the emotional peak**, not the opener. "Her name was Amy Eskridge, she was gone a month later" hits harder than "Amy Eskridge was working on..."
5. **On-screen text must be 2-4 words** with emoji. It complements the hook, it doesn't carry the video.

## EXAMPLE 4: Hantavirus Cruise Ship — First Video (Script Structure + TikTok Safety)

**STORY:** A rare virus spreading person-to-person on a cruise ship stuck in the Atlantic, with countries refusing to let it dock. First video in what became a massive series (4M, 10M, 26M views on subsequent videos).

---

### DRAFT 1 — Full script with Hook 2

HOOK: The WHO just confirmed they suspect a rare virus is spreading person to person on a cruise ship stuck in the Atlantic — and no country will let it dock.

SCRIPT:
So there are 149 people from 23 different countries trapped on this ship right now, including 17 Americans. Three passengers have already passed away — a Dutch couple and a German national — and one British passenger is currently in intensive care in South Africa. [continued...]

### NICK'S FEEDBACK
> "I have to be careful with saying passed away and saying death and stuff like that so that's my first note because of just algorithm and then the 149 people including 17 Americans the the 17 Americans angle is unnecessary just 149 people from 23 different countries trapped on the ship is fine"

> "the world health organization just said today that they believe it's spreading between people on the ship, which almost never happens, don't say which almost never happens. Only one strain in history is ever done that just say the fact that it is incredibly rare and what would happen if they find that that is the case"

> "and here's the part that makes this terrifying as fluff cut it. There's no cure there's no treatment there's no vaccine that's fine death rate again I can't say death"

> "Cape Verde said no you're not coming here. That sounds weird. Why would you say it like that"

**WHAT HE'S SAYING:**
- **TikTok algorithm sensitivity:** Can't say "passed away," "death," "death rate" — algorithm suppresses content with those words. Use alternatives like "gone," "didn't make it."
- **Cut unnecessary specifics:** "17 Americans" is an unnecessary detail that narrows the scope. "149 people from 23 countries" is broader and more impactful.
- **Don't editorialize transitions:** "here's the part that makes this terrifying" is fluff. Just state the fact.
- **Don't write dialogue for countries:** "Cape Verde said no, you're not coming here" sounds like a bad movie script.

### DRAFT 2 — Fixed TikTok language, cut fluff

### NICK'S FEEDBACK — Missing information
> "Three passengers are gone a Dutch couple and a German national and then a British passenger. I feel like all of that backstory is unnecessary. Three passengers are gone and one is an intensive care is fine, but didn't you just say that there were other suspected cases and stuff like include all of that you cut out so much from the angles and the bullet points"

**WHAT HE'S SAYING:** Cut the nationalities of the deceased (unnecessary detail), but DON'T cut the total case count. Include "at least three more suspected cases on board with two crew members showing symptoms" — pack in more facts, not fewer.

### FINAL APPROVED SCRIPT (after fix)

The fix was one paragraph: "Three passengers are already gone, one is in intensive care, and there are at least three more suspected cases on board with two crew members showing symptoms right now."

**Result: 4 million views in one day.**

**WHAT HE'S SAYING:** Every period = a camera cut in Nick's filming process. Short sentences like "Georgia has two." / "Arizona has one." / "Virginia has one." would mean 3 camera cuts in rapid succession, which is jarring and not how Nick films. Sentences must be 12-25 words.

## SUMMARY: NICK'S UNIVERSAL FEEDBACK PATTERNS

After analyzing all 8 examples, here are the patterns that appear in EVERY revision cycle:

### 1. THE "CNN" TEST
If a hook or script could appear as a news chyron on CNN, it fails. Nick is not a journalist. He's a personality telling a friend something insane. The vibe is FaceTime, not teleprompter.

### 2. ZERO FLUFF TOLERANCE
The following are ALWAYS cut:
- "But here's the catch..."
- "Here's the thing..."
- "Now this is where it gets crazy..."
- "Here's what's really wild..."
- "But here's what's making this so much harder..."
Any transitional phrase that adds no information gets cut. Go straight to the fact.

### 3. DON'T RESTATE THE HOOK
The first sentence of the script body must be NEW information. If the hook says "every guy in America is on the draft list" — don't open the script by explaining that every guy is on the draft list.

### 4. PRONUNCIATION IS A HARD GATE
If Nick can't say it out loud, it can't be in the script: Bundibugyo, DRC, Tenerife, Johannesburg, St. Helena — all get cut or rephrased. Use simple, pronounceable alternatives.

### 5. EVERY SENTENCE IS A CAMERA CUT
Every period = a camera cut. Sentences must be 12-25 words. Too short (3-5 words) = jarring rapid cuts. Too long (25+ words) = run-on that's hard to film.

### 6. UNIVERSAL UNDERSTANDING
Hook name-drops must be universally recognized: FBI, Pentagon, NASA, Congress = good. MIT, DARPA, DRC, Bundibugyo = bad. The average viewer scrolling TikTok must instantly understand every word.

### 7. CONNECT TO TRENDING
Always look for the bigger context. Military draft + Iran tensions = viral. Policy explainer alone = boring. The story is the excuse; the connection to what's trending is the engagement multiplier.

### 8. BRAND INTEGRATIONS ARE DATA POINTS
Kalshi mentions must read as evidence supporting the narrative, not ad breaks. "People are taking this so seriously that $30K is riding on it" = good. "On Kalshi right now, there's a market asking whether..." = ad.

### 9. SCALE OVER INDIVIDUALS
In global stories, don't isolate one person (one American doctor, one Dutch couple). The SCALE is what stops the scroll — "330 cases," "12 countries," "149 people trapped."

### 10. NICK EDITS HOOKS HIMSELF
When the AI can't nail the hook after multiple rounds, Nick often writes or edits it himself. The AI's job is to provide raw material and options; Nick's instinct for what "rolls off the tongue" is the final filter.
